What Fagotex is

Film-faced plywood (also sold in Europe as “tego” or phenolic plywood) is a structural panel whose faces are hot-pressed with resin-impregnated phenolic film. The film seals the face against water, chemicals and abrasion; a wire-mesh imprint on the working side adds anti-slip grip underfoot and under load.

Most film-faced panels on the market wrap that protective skin around a birch or mixed-species core. Fagotex puts it on 100% European beech — denser and harder than any birch panel — so the strength doesn’t stop at the skin. When a pallet truck drops a corner or a load shifts in transit, it’s the core that decides whether the floor survives. Ours is the hardest one available.

  • Heavy-duty packaging & workshop surfaces — where a raw face would wear, the film takes the abuse.

Why a beech core beats a birch core under film

Film-faced birch is the default in the market — but the film is only as good as what’s under it. Beech brings 750 vs ~650 kg/m³ density and a harder surface under the same film, which means fewer through-dents, tighter screw-holding at fixing points, and longer floor life on the same thickness. And unlike birch, the core is EU-origin with clean paperwork: FSC, E1, EUDR-compliant, REX registered. The full beech vs birch comparison is here.
